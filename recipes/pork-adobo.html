Pork Abodo

Description

Adobo is a iconic and beloved Filipino dish made of tender pork shoulder or belly slow-cooked in a savory, tangy, and aromatic sauce. As the pork simmers, it absorbs the rich blend of vinegar, soy sauce, garlic, and spices, resulting in a comforting dish that is best enjoyed served hot over a bowl of steamed white rice.

Ingredients

  • 2 lbs pork belly or pork shoulder, cut into bite-sized cubes
  • 1/2 cup soy sauce
  • 1/2 cup cane vinegar or white vinegar
  • 1 whole head of garlic, crushed and chopped
  • 1 medium yellow onion, sliced
  • 1 cup water or broth
  • 3 to 4 dried bay leaves
  • 1 tablespoon whole black peppercorns
  • 2 tablespoons cooking oil
  • Salt and sugar to taste (optional)

Steps

  1. Marinate the pork: In a bowl, combine the pork cubes, soy sauce, crushed garlic, and black peppercorns. Cover and let it marinate in the refrigerator for at least 30 minutes (preferably 1–2 hours for best flavor).
  2. Sear the meat: Heat cooking oil in a large pot or wok over medium-high heat. Remove the pork pieces from the marinade (reserve the liquid) and sear them for about 5 to 7 minutes until browned on all sides.
  3. Simmer: Pour the reserved marinade and 1 cup of water into the pot. Add the bay leaves. Bring the mixture to a boil, then reduce the heat to low, cover with a lid, and simmer for 30 to 40 minutes until the pork becomes tender.
  4. Add vinegar: Uncover the pot and pour in the vinegar. Allow it to boil for 3 to 5 minutes without stirring to let the harsh acidity cook off.
  5. Reduce the sauce: Stir everything together and let it continue simmering uncovered for another 10 to 15 minutes until the sauce reduces and thickens to your preferred consistency. Taste and adjust with a pinch of sugar or salt if needed.
  6. Serve: Remove the bay leaves, transfer to a serving dish, and serve hot with steaming white rice.
